Candidate Endorsements

City Council District 1: Ross Bernet #1, Daria Wrubel #2

We endorse both Ross Bernet and Daria Wrubel, and recommend ranking them in that order on the ballot. Both Ross and Daria share our conviction for the need to make Berkeley a welcoming, climate-friendly city, and the critical role of new housing of all levels in realizing this vision. They also align with our views on critical upcoming policy decisions, notably approving the compromise Commercial Zoning proposal from Planning Commission and implementing the approved approach for the Hopkins bike route. We strongly recommend ranking Ross and Daria above all other candidates in the ranked-choice ballot.

City Council District 4: Igor Tregub

We endorse Igor Tregub, who stands head and shoulders above his opponent in the district 4 council race and we endorse him without reservation. Igor has a long, admirable record as a climate-change activist, and as a council member his understanding of the interrelationship of climate, housing and transportation policies has evolved appreciably. In alignment with our views he ultimately voted to advance the Hopkins bike route and supported the compromise Commercial Zoning proposal from the Planning Commission.

Igor is a consensus-builder and incrementalist. This is often a strength, but in a new term, we hope Igor will take decisive, and when needed unpopular, positions, and become a leader in confronting Berkeley’s greatest climate challenge: our reliance on personal, gasoline-powered vehicles.

City Council District 7: Syrak Mikael

We endorse Syrak Micael, who we believe will continue the strong legacy of Cecilia Lunapara and Rigel Robinson. His questionnaire showed that he is adept at connecting the dots when it comes to housing, transportation, and the climate. Not only does he acknowledge local drivers of climate change, he also knows what a city can do and he supports those policies. That last part is important, as our city's leaders need to meaningfully support realistic policies in order to help our city do its best to fight climate change. During the Future of Berkeley forum on 9/10/26, Syrak’s answers were consistent with his stances on the questionnaire and continued to show support for safe streets, active transportation, and dense, transit-oriented housing. Therefore, Micael has earned BNHCA's endorsement for Berkeley City Council District 7.

City Council District 8: Mark Humbert

We are delighted to endorse Mark Humbert for reelection in District 8. Mark has shown both that he shares many of our beliefs, and also that he has enormous courage. He has advocated effectively during his first term for fair housing and progress toward Berkeley’s ambitious climate impact goals. Mark voted to add fair housing programs to our 2023 Housing Element Update, and diligently pursued those programs by shepherding the Middle Housing Zoning and crafting the Corridor Zoning Update that was passed by the Planning Commission. These programs are already bringing new, sustainable, infill homes to neighborhoods across Berkeley. Mark has also consistently focused on sustainable transportation, working with state leaders and regional agencies to improve traffic safety, align AC Transit bus service with Berkeley’s needs, ensure that the sidewalk repair budget is funded, and to rapidly comply with the new intersection daylighting rules passed by the state. Mark was also a vocal proponent of the successful 2024 Measure FF, which doubled funding for street safety and accelerated re-paving. Bringing more homes to Berkeley and making our streets safe for sustainable transportation are the key pillars of Berkeley’s climate change strategy, and Mark has more than earned a repeat endorsement for Berkeley City Council District 8.

Ballot Measure Endorsements

Measure RTM: Yes

While BNHCA normally doesn’t endorse measures that go beyond Berkeley, this is a critical exception. We recommend voting YES on Measure RTM. Berkeley’s walkable neighborhoods are built around, and enabled by, our rich public transit network. But a sharp drop-off in ridership after the COVID-19 pandemic has left our transit agencies dangerously short on funding to continue operating. Without Measure RTM funding, BART would have to shut down two lines, close 15 stations, and cut service by 70%. At the same time, AC Transit would have to cut service 16%. This would make it harder to get around without a car, jeopardize our climate action goals, increase traffic and gridlock, and move Berkeley—and the entire Bay Area—in the wrong direction. Vote YES on Measure RTM!

We take no stance on the Berkeley measures, as none are directly relevant to our core mission.

Mayor

Adena Ishii for Mayor of Berkeley. The role of Mayor is to chair Council, and we believe Adena would be highly effective. Her personal experience with housing insecurity and bike/traffic danger would be very relevant to future decisions.

City Council

Terry Taplin for Council District 2. Terry has been a strong and effective leader on city council, supporting housing and street safety. He deserves a second term. We appreciate graduate student Jenny Guarino’s entry into city politics, find many of her views similar to ours, and hope she continues to be involved.

We support both Deborah Matthews and Ben Bartlett for Council District 3. There are 3 candidates in this race. Both incumbent Ben Bartlett and challenger Deborah Matthews have strengths that we respect. Deborah has been a leader in organizing for housing at Ashby BART. Ben is a capable and experienced leader who has been willing to listen to pro-housing advocates throughout his tenure.

Todd Andrew for Council District 5. Todd has advocated for affordable, transit-oriented housing and green transportation for years. Todd supports Measure FF, the Safe Streets Initiative. His opponent in the District 5 race, Shoshana O’Keefe, is the only candidate or public figure in Berkeley to endorse Measure EE, the car-centric paving plan that BNHCA opposes. We believe Todd Andrew to be the better representative for street safety and transportation for District 5.

Brent Blackaby for Council District 6. Brent is a strong new voice at the city level. He has a balanced but clear view in favor of housing and climate action, and is endorsed by many, including Councilmember Wengraf, who we endorsed and respect. We appreciate Andy’s views and see similar alignment with our goals, but on balance believe that Brent would be a more effective Councilmember.

City Measures

Measure W: No position.

Measure W increases the taxes paid when real property is transferred to new owners. BNHCA takes no position for or against transfer taxes in general, but we feel that Measure W could be better written and it is not necessary to change the tax this year. The existing transfer tax does not expire until 2029, and Measure W would not go into effect until 2027, so there is plenty of time for the city to refine the proposal. We would prefer a transfer tax that exempts the first sale of new multi-family housing.

Measure AA: Yes

Under the California state constitution, voters must explicitly authorize spending above levels set almost 40 years ago (inflation adjusted) regardless of how costs of services have risen and without regard to our separate votes for individual taxes to support parks, libraries, emergency services, disabled and homeless services and so on. This so-called Gann Limit, a legacy of the “tax revolt” of the late 1970s and early 1980s forces this bit of red tape on us, the voters. Measure AA does not create any new taxes. It simply authorizes the city to spend for purposes we have previously authorized for the next four years, until we have to go through this rigmarole again. Thank you Howard Jarvis and Paul Gann.

Measures BB and CC: No on both

It’s true that Berkeley’s rent control ordinance could be better. We’d like to see amendments that encourage the creation and availability of more rental units, protect tenants from negligent landlords, and ensure that evictions for nonpayment of rent are for material amounts. However, both measures make a host of other changes that are either unnecessary or counterproductive. Overall, it appears unlikely that either measure would have significant benefits on net, so we recommend no on both measures and hope to support a better set of amendments in 2026.

Measure EE: No

Measure EE fails to make necessary investments in our walking and biking infrastructure. It fails to provide adequate funding to maintain our streets, and it even fails to provide effective accountability and oversight, despite that being a major talking point of the EE proponents. It’s a poorly drafted alternative to Measure FF intended to block the passage of FF in order to avoid funding new safety infrastructure for people walking and biking. Vote NO on EE and YES on FF.

Measure FF: Yes

Measure FF is the only measure on the ballot that invests in citywide safety improvements for people walking and biking, while also helping to fix Berkeley’s crumbling streets and sidewalks. It’s critical that we make walking and biking safer in order to encourage these alternative forms of transportation and reduce reliance on automobiles. Measure FF is also endorsed by Mayor Arreguin and 7 of 8 current members of the city council, Assembly Member Buffy Wicks, the Sierra Club, the League of Women Voters, the Berkeley Democratic Club and many other individuals and organizations. Vote YES on FF and NO on EE.

Measure GG: No

At BNHCA, we believe decarbonization must be a primary policy goal for society. While measure GG appears consistent with that goal, it is poorly designed and would have many bad effects and few if any good ones. It would tax some of the most efficient energy users in the city and likely chase out many businesses, merely causing them to move to other nearby locations, as well as accelerating the closure of already precarious arts & music nonprofits and possibly of Alta Bates hospital.

There are many parts to a well designed decarbonization pathway for Berkeley and we will continue to advocate for them. The City Council is the appropriate elected body to evaluate all the competing considerations to create effective policies and adjust them as needed over time. A poorly crafted and rigid initiative is not the right way forward. (We feel similarly about HH incidentally, but it's only tangentially related to our agenda.)
